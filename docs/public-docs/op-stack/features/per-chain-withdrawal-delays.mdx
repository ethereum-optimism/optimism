Before Upgrade 22, every standard OP Stack chain waited the same fixed time before a withdrawal could finalize on L1.
The delays that make up that wait were constants in the shared contract implementations.
Upgrade 22 moves them into each chain's own contracts.
A chain can then set its own delays within bounds that the implementation fixes.
With every delay at its lower bound, a withdrawal can finalize about one day after it is proven, which is why this feature is often called *1-day withdrawals*.

The upgrade itself does not shorten any delay.
It carries each chain's current values into the new contracts unchanged.
Shortening a delay is a separate, deliberate action by the chain's L1 ProxyAdmin owner.

<Warning>
  The withdrawal delays are the time your chain has to catch and stop an invalid withdrawal before it finalizes.
  Shortening them can shrink that time to as little as 12 hours.
  Optimism strongly discourages shortening a chain's delays without redundant security monitoring and 24/7 incident response in place.
  Read [Before you shorten a chain's delays](#before-you-shorten-a-chains-delays) first.
</Warning>

## The three delays

| Delay | Contract | What it holds back | Lower bound | Upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proof maturity delay | `OptimismPortal2` | Finalizing a withdrawal, counted from when it was proven | 1 day | 7 days |
| Dispute game finality delay | `AnchorStateRegistry` | Treating a resolved dispute game as final, counted from when it resolved | 12 hours | 3.5 days |
| Bond withdrawal delay | `DelayedWETH` | Withdrawing dispute game bonds, counted from when they were unlocked | 12 hours | 7 days |

For the proof maturity and finality delays, the upper bounds are the standard values from before Upgrade 22, so these delays cannot be made longer than they were.
The lower bounds are chosen so that a chain using every minimum still has a one-day withdrawal time.
The bounds are immutables of the contract implementations.
Moving them requires deploying new implementations, which for a governed chain means a governance-approved upgrade.

The [configurability specification](https://specs.optimism.io/protocol/configurability.html) lists these ranges as the standard values for each parameter.

## How the delays set a withdrawal's wait

A withdrawal proven against a dispute game can finalize only when, among other checks, all of these hold:

* The proof maturity delay has passed since the withdrawal was proven.
* The dispute game has resolved in favor of the proposal, and the dispute game finality delay has passed since it resolved.
* The game was of the respected game type when it was created, and the Guardian has not blacklisted or retired it.
* Withdrawals are not paused.

A shorter proof maturity delay does not make every withdrawal faster.
A withdrawal also waits for its dispute game to resolve, and for the finality delay after that.
How long a game takes to resolve depends on the game type, not on these delays.

Lowering the delays is intended for chains whose games resolve on a fixed, short schedule: permissioned chains and chains using the [ZK dispute game](/op-stack/zk-proofs/zk-dispute-game).
Chains using the permissionless fault dispute game are expected to keep a finality delay of 3.5 days, so that the game clock and the finality delay still total 7 days.

The bond withdrawal delay does not affect user withdrawals.
It protects dispute game bonds instead: it is the time the L1 ProxyAdmin owner has to recover bonds from `DelayedWETH` if a game paid them to the wrong party.
A Guardian pause also blocks bond withdrawals in the meantime.

## How a chain changes its delays

Only the chain's L1 ProxyAdmin owner can change a delay.
Each contract has its own setter, which rejects any value outside the bounds:

| Delay | Setter | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof maturity delay | `OptimismPortal2.setProofMaturityDelaySeconds` | `ProofMaturityDelaySecondsSet` |
| Dispute game finality delay | `AnchorStateRegistry.setDisputeGameFinalityDelaySeconds` | `DisputeGameFinalityDelaySecondsSet` |
| Bond withdrawal delay | `DelayedWETH.setDelay` | `DelaySet` |

A new value takes effect immediately for everything already in flight.
Lowering the proof maturity delay applies to withdrawals that were proven before the change.
Lowering the finality delay applies to games that resolved before the change.
Lowering the bond withdrawal delay applies to bonds that were unlocked before the change.
This is why the order of operations in [Rules for changing the delays](#rules-for-changing-the-delays) matters.

Chains in an interop set share one `AnchorStateRegistry` and one `DelayedWETH`, so they share one finality delay and one bond withdrawal delay.
Each chain's `OptimismPortal2` keeps its own proof maturity delay.
Keep the proof maturity delay the same on every chain in an interop set: the set's pooled ETH can leave through whichever chain has the shortest one.

The `OPContractsManagerStandardValidator` accepts any value within the bounds.

## Before you shorten a chain's delays

The contracts are designed to reject every invalid withdrawal.
The withdrawal delays exist for the case where that design fails, for example through a contract bug or an invalid dispute game that resolves as valid.
During the delays, monitoring has time to notice the problem and the Guardian has time to pause withdrawals and blacklist or retire the game.
Once an invalid withdrawal finalizes, the funds it takes are gone.

With every delay at its lower bound, a withdrawal can finalize one day after it is proven, and as little as 12 hours after its dispute game resolves.
If the problem only becomes visible when the game resolves, 12 hours is all the time your monitoring has to detect it and your responders have to stop it.
Run on short delays only if detection and response fit inside that window.
They must fit at any hour, and with one component failed.

What follows is the minimum bar.
What your chain needs depends on its configuration and changes as the protocol evolves.

### A proof system that resolves on a fixed schedule

Shorter delays only make sense when dispute games resolve quickly and predictably.
See [How the delays set a withdrawal's wait](#how-the-delays-set-a-withdrawals-wait).

### Withdrawal monitoring with redundancy

* **Check every proven withdrawal against L2.**
  For each withdrawal proven to the `OptimismPortal2` contract, confirm that it was initiated in the `L2ToL1MessagePasser` contract on L2.
  [`op-dispute-mon`](/chain-operators/tools/chain-monitoring#dispute-mon) does not do this: it checks dispute games, not individual withdrawals.
* **Run at least two independent monitors for this check.**
  Use separate code, separate infrastructure, and separate paging paths, so that one bug, outage, or compromised account cannot blind both.
* **Read from several independent RPC providers.**
  Use more than one L1 and more than one L2 provider for each monitor, each run by a different operator.
  Require them to agree, and treat a failed or conflicting read as an alert, never as "withdrawal found."
  A single provider that returns wrong data can hide an invalid withdrawal.
* **Cover every withdrawal that can still finalize.**
  A monitor that restarts must rebuild enough history to see every proof that is still pending.
* **Alert when a monitor goes quiet.**
  A monitor that stops checking fails silently. Page on missing heartbeats or stale checks, not only on findings.
* **Watch the proof system and the configuration.**
  Run `op-dispute-mon` for dispute games, and alert on every pause and every `ProofMaturityDelaySecondsSet`, `DisputeGameFinalityDelaySecondsSet`, and `DelaySet` event from your chain's contracts.

### Incident response that fits inside the window

* **Pages reach a responder at any hour.**
  Alerts page an on-call responder 24/7, and an unacknowledged page escalates to a second responder.
  Test that a page from each monitor is delivered before you rely on that monitor.
* **Pause first, investigate second.**
  A pause stops finalization while you work out what happened.
  The response policy should require a pause for a critical withdrawal alert, without triage.
  Expect some pauses to turn out to be false alarms: that is the cost of a short window.
* **The pause path works when you need it.**
  Responders can reach the [Guardian](/op-stack/protocol/privileged-roles#guardian) or pause key, the account that sends the pause is funded, and the tooling and runbooks are available even if your usual services are down.
  Plan for a pause transaction that is slow to land on L1, and know how to resend it.
* **Drill it on this chain.**
  Run the full detect, page, and pause sequence on this chain before shortening its delays, and repeat it on a schedule.

### Rules for changing the delays

Because a new value applies to everything already in flight, follow these rules for every change:

* **Do not lower a delay while a security alert is open**, and never while a known-invalid game has resolved.
  Lowering the delay could make withdrawals proven against that game finalizable at once.
* **Do not lower a delay while withdrawals are paused.**
  A shorter delay set during a pause can make waiting withdrawals finalizable the moment the chain unpauses.
* **Set the proof maturity delay of every chain in an interop set in the same transaction**, to the same value.
  The finality and bond withdrawal delays are already shared, because the set shares one `AnchorStateRegistry` and one `DelayedWETH`.
* **Raising a delay is the safe direction.**
  If anything looks wrong after a change, raise the delays back while you investigate.

Chains governed through `superchain-ops` change their delays with the `SetReviewPeriodDelays` template.
Before signing, it checks that each value is within bounds, that no portal in the task is paused, and that an interop set keeps one proof maturity delay.
It cannot check for open security alerts, so that confirmation stays manual.
