title: "Derivation in Kona Node"
diataxis: explanation
---

The `DerivationActor` runs the `kona-derive` pipeline to turn canonical L1 inputs into L2
payload attributes. It uses the engine owner's atomic snapshot and acknowledged results
to choose the parent for its next pipeline step.

## Pipeline context

Derivation starts after the engine reaches its ready phase. Before stepping, it resets the
pipeline to the accepted pending-safe parent, which equals completed safe after an engine
reset. Only a successful reset installs that epoch and parent as the pipeline context. Temporary reset failures retry before further stepping;
critical failures stop the node. Repeated local resets without accepted L2 progress back off
from one second to twelve seconds. A newer engine epoch supersedes that wait. Prepared attributes left in the pipeline's output queue are discarded after reset.

Every newer engine reset epoch invalidates pending attributes, pipeline context and the
engine owner's finality records, even if the safe head has not changed. A pipeline-only
reset preserves accepted finality records in the engine. An interrupted pipeline step is followed
by a complete successful reset before the pipeline is used again.

## Applying attributes

The actor sends attributes with the context under which they were produced and waits for
an engine acknowledgement. A temporary failure retains those exact attributes and context.
An obsolete acknowledgement cannot install a parent or flush a channel. The engine records
accepted completed-safe work before replying, even if derivation abandons that reply.

An `Applied` result advances the context to the actual accepted pending-safe parent.
Before Holocene a span prefix remains pending until its concluding element succeeds. `Replaced`
also requires a successful channel flush before another pipeline step. `Dropped` consumes
the rejected batch and follows the returned pending parent, without adding a finality record
or requesting a flush. A rewound parent makes the batch queue discard the invalid span remainder.

## Finality

The engine owner records the actual completed-safe L2 block hash and its L1 inclusion
source when consolidation applies or replaces a block. Pending span prefixes, dropped
batches and bootstrap trust create no record. New records and the shared finalized-L1
observation both trigger finalization, including while derivation is idle.

The owner validates the observed finalized L1 signal, inclusion source and canonical L2
hash before sending finalized forkchoice. Temporary read failures retain records and retry
independently after 100 milliseconds. Ambiguous forkchoice failures or `SYNCING` require
engine recovery; only `VALID` publishes the proposed finalized head.

## Inputs and delegation

The L1 watcher sends head wakeups to derivation and publishes independently observed L1
head, safe and finalized blocks for engine and RPC readers. Engine
readiness, reset and safe-state changes arrive through the shared snapshot; there is no
engine-to-derivation callback queue.

Delegated derivation polls an upstream node, checks its L1 references against canonical L1,
and passes its actual safe and trusted finalized blocks together through `follow_safe`
under the captured epoch. The reply acknowledges safe acceptance; finalized completion
is observed through the engine snapshot. A trusted claim retained during EL sync is
applied only after the engine becomes ready, without inventing local inclusion records.

See the [derivation specification](https://specs.optimism.io/protocol/derivation.html) for
protocol rules and the [engine design](./engine) for execution ownership and recovery.

The actor publishes `current_l1` from the pipeline's actual origin, independently of observed
L1 heads and completed L2 safe progress. Invalidated context reports unknown progress until a
successful reset. Delegation publishes each validated upstream progress observation even when
its safe head is unchanged or local application must retry.
